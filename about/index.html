Who are we?

Two people who got tired of waiting for somebody else to build this.

This is the longer version. If you only want the short one: we find the money your operation is losing in the gaps, and then we build the systems that close them. The rest of this page is why we care, which matters more than it sounds like it should.

We want you to get to Friday and not be able to name the thing you dropped.

That is the whole idea. Not a bigger business. Not a leaner one. Not a number on a dashboard you look at twice and then stop opening. Just a week where the thing you forgot was not expensive, because it never depended on you remembering it in the first place.

Nobody can promise you that. Anyone who does is selling you something. We think it is worth building toward anyway, and we would rather say it like that than dress it up.

Brandon

I kept finding the same hole and could not work out why nobody had filled it.

Most of my working life has been on productions and inside small operations, and every single one of them was losing money somewhere nobody was looking. Not through incompetence. Through Tuesday. The quote that never got chased because a truck broke down. The invoice that went out nine days late because the one person who sends them was on a job all week. The customer from two years ago who would happily come back and has not heard from anyone since.

None of that shows up on a P&L as a line item, so nobody fixes it, and it keeps happening every week for years.

Every time I found one I assumed somebody must already be selling the fix. Every time I looked, they were selling something adjacent to it: a piece of software you still have to operate, or a service that handles one narrow slice and hands the rest back. So we built the thing I kept expecting to find.

Andrea

I like people. I do not like watching work grind them down for no reason.

I run branding and creative for a living, which means I have sat across from a lot of owners at exactly the point where they are excellent at the thing they actually do and drowning in everything bolted onto it. The part that gets me is that almost none of what is drowning them is the work. It is the paperwork about the work, the chasing about the work, the retyping of the work into a second system.

Watching someone genuinely good at something get worn down by a task a machine would do without ever complaining is a specific kind of annoying. That is the part I am here to delete.

You keep the controls. That is not a slogan.

The nervous version of this conversation always goes the same way: so you are going to replace my people. No. The opposite, and there is a well-known example of why.

Toyota builds cars on some of the most heavily automated lines in the world, and they still put a cord within reach of every person on that line. Anyone can pull it and stop production. They call the idea automation with a human element, and the reason it exists is that the machine is excellent at doing the same thing ten thousand times and hopeless at noticing that this particular one is wrong. The person is the opposite. So the machine runs, and the person keeps the authority.

You are not handing anything over. You are giving certain jobs to something that does not get tired, and keeping the ones that need a person who does.

Practically, that means you can turn any piece of it off. You can read every message that goes out before it goes out. If something we built is doing the wrong thing, you tell us and we change it that week. Nothing we build is allowed to become a thing you cannot see inside of.

What we will not do

  • We will not guess at a price before we have looked. A number we made up is worse than no number at all, and it anchors the whole conversation to fiction.
  • We will not tell you how to run your business. You already know. We are looking at one specific thing: where the work leaks out between the people doing it.
  • We will not pretend a machine is a person to your customers. Where something does talk to them, it says what it is, and it will not diagnose a problem or quote a price it has no business quoting.
  • We will not lock you in. Month to month, thirty days' notice, and if you ever leave, the phone number goes with you.
  • We will tell you on the first call if we cannot help you. Before you have paid anything. That call costs you half an hour and you will still leave knowing what your gaps cost.

The part we are not going to be coy about

This is not the only thing we are building. There is a drinks brand, film work, and a sustainability project behind it, and this company is the one that has to work first. We are not embarrassed about that and we are not going to pretend otherwise in a mission statement.

It matters to you for one reason: we are not trying to become the biggest agency in Los Angeles. We are trying to do a small number of engagements extremely well, because the ones that work are the ones that pay for everything else. That makes us easy to fire and unusually motivated not to be.

If that reads as too honest, that is fine. Take it or leave it. If it reads as the sort of people you would rather hand this to, the calendar is below.
