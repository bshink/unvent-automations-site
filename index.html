The part everyone worries about

You keep the controls.

Every conversation about handing work to a machine reaches the same moment. The owner leans back a bit and says some version of: so what you are telling me is I lose control of my own business.

Reasonable thing to worry about. Wrong worry. The cleanest counter-example has been running since the 1950s.

Toyota automated the line, then put a cord next to every person standing at it. Anyone can pull it. The line stops.

The machine

Perfect ten thousand times. Completely blind to the one that came out wrong.

The person

Bad at ten thousand. Very good at the one.

The machine does the repetition. You keep the authority. Nobody at Toyota confused those two jobs, which is why they were still building the good cars while everyone else argued about whether robots were coming for the line. Every line we build has a cord on it.

Before you book

The questions everybody asks.

How much does this cost?

Builds start at $3,500. What moves that number is how many things we are fixing and how tangled what you have now is. On top of the build there is a monthly retainer that scales with how much we are running for you. We will not quote you before we have looked at your operation, because we would be guessing, and a number we made up is worse than no number at all.

How fast is it running?

The first pieces are live in about a week. We start with whatever we can prove is losing you money fastest, usually the phone, and build out from there.

Do you need access to my software?

Usually less than people expect. A lot of what we build runs alongside what you already have rather than inside it. Where we do need to connect something, we tell you exactly what and why before it gets built, and you can say no to any piece of it. Nothing gets access it does not need. If a fix genuinely requires writing into your dispatch, booking or accounting system, that is a separate conversation and a separate quote, and we would tell you that up front rather than after.

Is it going to sound like a robot to my customers?

Most of what we build your customers never see or hear at all. Invoicing, scheduling, reporting and the handoffs between your own people all happen behind the scenes. Where something does talk to a customer, it answers immediately, it knows what you sell and what you charge, and it asks what your front desk would ask. It will not pretend to be something it is not, diagnose a problem, or quote a price. And if your customers would genuinely be better served by a person on that line, we will tell you that.

What if it does not work for my business?

The agreement is month to month and either of us can end it with thirty days' notice. You are not signing up for a year. The setup fee covers work that has already been done, so it is not refundable once we have started, and we would rather say that plainly now than surprise you with it later. If we do not think we can help you, we will tell you on the first call, before you have paid anything.

Am I locked in?

No. Month to month, thirty days' notice, and if you ever leave, the phone number we set up gets ported to you. You keep it. We are not going to hold your number hostage to keep your business.

We want you to get to Friday and not be able to name the thing you dropped.

Not a bigger business, not a leaner one, not a number on a dashboard. Just a week where the thing you forgot was not expensive, because it did not depend on you remembering it.
